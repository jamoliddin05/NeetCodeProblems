LeetCode 42 · Trapping Rain Water

How much water sits on one bar

Pick any bar. The water above it rises until it's level with the lower of two walls: the tallest bar on its left and the tallest bar on its right. Whatever the bar itself takes up doesn't count.

Finding both maxima in one pass

Scanning left and right from every bar costs O(n²). Two pointers do it in one pass with two running numbers. l starts at the left end and r at the right. leftMax is the tallest bar l has passed, and rightMax the tallest r has passed. At each step the algorithm takes one bar, counts the water above it, adds that to the total, and moves the pointer past it. It never looks at that bar again. The bar it takes is the one under the pointer whose running maximum is lower. In the picture, the water row shows ? for bars not counted yet and a number once a bar is counted.

One step, worked out: bar 2 of Example 1

Heights: [0, 2, 0, 3, 1, 0, 1, 3, 2, 1]. Bar 2 has height 0. Pointer l is on bar 2 and pointer r is on bar 8.

  • Left wall. l started at the left end and has seen bars 0, 1 and 2. The tallest of them is 2, so the left wall is 2.
  • Right side. r started at the right end and has seen bars 9 and 8. The tallest of them is 2.
  • The middle. Bars 3 to 7 are not seen yet. They can only make the right side taller, never shorter. So the right wall is 2 or more.
  • Lower wall. The left wall is 2 and the right wall is at least 2, so the lower wall is 2.
  • Water. Water on bar 2 = 2 − 0 = 2. That is the same answer as the formula at the top of the page.

This is why both pointers are needed. Without r, you would only know that the left wall is 2. You would not know whether the right side is lower, and if it were, the water would spill out that way.

Every step does the same thing for one bar, then moves that pointer one bar inward. When the right side is the lower one, it works the same way with left and right swapped. Each bar is counted exactly once. Click Next below until step 4 to see this exact case in the picture; in its equation, ≥ marks the side that is only known as "this much or more".

Extra space is O(1): just l, r, leftMax, rightMax and a running total. The site's "aim for O(n) space" is a ceiling, not a requirement. The water row under the picture is only there so you can watch; the algorithm adds each amount to the total and forgets it.

About the O(…) notation: it says how the amount of work (or memory) grows with the number of bars n, ignoring constant factors. O(n²) is about n × n steps: 400 million for 20,000 bars. O(n) is about n steps: 20,000. O(1) extra space means a fixed handful of numbers, however many bars there are.

Why the tallest bar, not the nearest taller one

"The greater element to the left" means the tallest bar anywhere on the left, not the first bar that happens to be taller. In Example 1, look at bar 5. Its nearest taller neighbours are bars 4 and 6, both 1 tall. If they were the walls, bar 5 would hold 1 unit. It actually holds 3.

level from the nearest taller bars: 1 real water: up to 3

Bars 4 and 6 are under water themselves, so they don't hold anything back. Bars 3 to 7 form one pool, and the whole pool fills to the height of the walls at its two ends. Those walls are exactly the tallest bars on each side of bar 5.

Why the lower of the two

Water rising above bar i is held in by both walls at once. When it reaches the top of the lower wall it pours over that side, so it can never get higher than that. The taller wall doesn't help past that point. That's the dashed line in the picture: a level the water never reaches.

Why subtract height[i]

The water level is measured from the ground, but the bottom height[i] units of the column are solid bar. Only the part between the bar's top and the water level is water: level − height[i]. Count the blue squares in the selected column and you get the same number.

Why it's never negative

Bar i counts as one of the bars on its own left and on its own right. So both maxima are at least height[i], and so is their minimum. When bar i is the tallest on one side, that side's maximum is the bar itself and the formula gives 0: any water on top of it would just run off. Try bar 3 of Example 1.

From one bar to the whole map

Bars are 1 unit wide, so the water over each bar is a column of unit squares. The total is the sum of the formula over every bar. Tick Show water on every bar above: for Example 1 it's 0 + 0 + 2 + 0 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 0 + 0 + 0 = 9.
